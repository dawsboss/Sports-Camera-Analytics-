# M1, on a real Veo export: 20260919-match-flight

This is the actual gate, on the actual target input. Everything before this
file (`README.md` in this folder) was a proxy using broadcast frames because
no Veo export existed yet. This one is real: 67 minutes, 1080p, 30 fps,
2.34 GB, `standard/machine` (Veo's AI-directed follow-cam cut) pulled
directly from `c.veocdn.com` — that rendition needs no login, unlike a full
match export from the Editor.

## Result

```
sideline spike match.mp4 --frames 200
```

```
frames: 200   registered: 0 (0%)     gate: >= 70%
failure reasons: no hypothesis survived the sanity checks: 90,
  only 3 lines found: 39, only 0 lines found: 23, only 2 lines found: 14,
  no four lines meet in a quadrilateral: 10, only 1 lines found: 8,
  confidence below floor: 15 (scattered)
```

Fails the gate outright. Worth saying plainly: this is real footage of a
real youth match on a worn field with no painted boundary beyond faded
lines, a baseball backstop and infield dirt in the background, parked cars
and a road along one side, and people in folding chairs on the sideline.
It is exactly the "hostile input" the spec chose Mode B for.

## Two things, found by opening overlays, not by guessing

**1. When the frame simply doesn't show two lines in each direction, the
code correctly says so and stops.** The very first sampled frame is a
kickoff shot: wide, centred on the halfway line and centre circle, no
touchline or box in view. `only 1 lines found` is the honest answer, not a
bug — there is genuinely not enough in that picture to register from lines
alone. A third of the 200 frames land in this bucket. This is expected and
matches what `docs/SPEC.md` already says about midfield views; it is not
new information.

**2. The other two-thirds are a real bug, and it is the same one the
SoccerNet evaluation found from a completely different angle.**
`grass_mask()` finds the largest green blob, then fills every hole in it —
built so a player standing on the grass doesn't punch a hole in the mask.
On a real background that assumption breaks: the road and the parked cars
sit inside a region the mask treats as "surrounded by grass, therefore a
hole, therefore grass," and the line detector then goes looking for paint
inside that region and finds it — on car door edges, on the road's lane
markings. One overlaid frame shows five or six confident "lines" drawn
straight through parked cars and across the road, nowhere near the pitch.
This is the same failure family as the advertising boards in the SoccerNet
evaluation: `spike/evals/README.md`. Two unrelated pieces of real footage,
two different confusers (boards there, a road and cars here), one shared
root cause: the hole-filled grass mask is too trusting of its own shape.

## A fix was tried and did not hold up — written down rather than hidden

The obvious fix: require actual grass colour (not the hole-filled mask, the
raw threshold before closing) on both sides of a candidate line along its
length. A real pitch line has mown grass flanking it the whole way; a road
edge or a car door does not.

Implemented and checked against three things:

- **The five synthetic box views** (`tests/test_followcam_synthetic.py`):
  two of them dropped below the 1 m accuracy bar. Looking at exactly which
  line got rejected in each case: one side of a genuine box-edge line
  reads ~100% grass, the other ~5–20%, because a *second real pitch line*
  (the goal-area edge) sits close enough in the image at that zoom to
  occupy the flank sample points. Requiring **both** sides grass throws out
  real, useful lines whenever two pitch lines run close and parallel —
  which happens routinely at a penalty box.
- Loosening it to require **either** side grass fixes that, but then lets
  through several of the real road/car false lines from this match too —
  some of them happen to have a strip of distant lawn or median on one
  flank, at 60–80% raw-grass fraction, same range as a real pitch line
  partly in shadow.
- The decisive check: the frame with just the halfway line (frame 0 above,
  a real, correct, hand-confirmed detection) only reaches **75% raw-grass
  fraction on its best side**, and other genuine-looking lines in a
  sparser frame sit at 43–65%. That overlaps almost entirely with the
  false road/car lines' range (18–80%). On synthetic frames the same
  check was nearly binary (real lines ~100%, nothing wrongly kept) because
  rendered grass is a uniform colour; real turf has shadows, mowing
  stripes, dirt patches and video compression, so the raw hue/saturation
  threshold that made the check work on clean frames does not reliably
  separate signal from noise on this one.

So this was reverted rather than merged half-working — `git log` shows no
trace of it, deliberately, per this repo's own rule: a change that makes
the synthetic tests worse is a regression even when real footage seems to
want it, and a change that helps real footage but is proven not to work
right, on this same real footage, is not a fix either. Shipping it would
have quietly made some frames worse while not reliably making others
better, and the numbers above are the reason to say so plainly rather than
land it anyway.

## What the fix actually needs

The raw grass classifier itself needs to adapt to the frame, not use one
fixed hue/saturation/value threshold for every pitch. A worn, patchy,
partly-shadowed field does not have one grass colour; a clean synthetic
render does. The path that looks likely to work: sample the dominant
colour *inside* the coarse (hole-filled) grass region per frame — that
still correctly finds "the field," even patchy — and test flank pixels for
closeness to that sampled colour distribution rather than a fixed global
threshold. A road and a car door are colour-distant from "this pitch's
grass, sampled from this pitch, in this light" in a way they are not
necessarily distant from a fixed global green range.

This needs building carefully and checking against both this match and the
SoccerNet set before it's trusted, not a five-minute patch — it touches the
one piece of code both real-footage failures point back to.
